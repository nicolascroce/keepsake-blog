Keepsake's July update is live. It brings one of the product's most awaited features — the time block system — and takes your notes a step further: publishing to your WordPress blog, links between notes, full-screen writing. Here's the tour.

## Your day in blocks, not schedules

A calendar asks: "What will you do at 2pm?" That's the wrong question. The right one: "What comes next?"

The Today view now carries a <mark>timeline of time blocks</mark>. You set your wake time, you stack your blocks — writing, sport, meetings, your tasks — each with a duration. Hours compute themselves. A block runs long? Everything after it shifts, nothing breaks. You finish early? You're free.

This isn't a visual gimmick; it's a planning philosophy:

- **Hours are derived, not decreed.** Move a block and every hour recomputes before your eyes, while you drag. For real appointments, give a block a fixed time: it anchors, and the sequence resumes after it.
- **Your tasks get their slot.** The day's tasks live in a dedicated block — a window, not a copy. Create several slots in the day: whatever isn't done in one carries over to the next, the way you'd migrate entries in a bullet journal.
- **A day has an end.** Set your bed time and the timeline shows the free time you have left — or how far your plan overshoots. It's the honest answer to an overstuffed day.
- **You always know where you stand.** A thin red line marks the present moment; a marker shows your next free slot.

Tasks can now also carry a time (and a duration) next to their date: a timed task anchors on the timeline, an untimed one stays comfortably in the tasks block. [Read the full time blocks guide →](https://keepsake.place/en/help/time-blocks)

## Publish your notes to your WordPress blog

You keep a WordPress blog? Connect it once from Settings — your site simply asks you to authorize "Keepsake", no password to copy. From then on, any note can become a blog post.

A small window lets you adjust the title, choose between <mark>draft, immediate or scheduled publication</mark>, and pick your blog's categories (or create new ones on the fly). Your images are transferred to your site's media library — the post doesn't depend on Keepsake to display. And if you rework the note later, the same button updates the post; never a duplicate.

Writing stays in Keepsake; publishing happens on your blog. [Connect your WordPress blog →](https://keepsake.place/en/help/connect-wordpress)

## Link your notes together

Your best ideas come from rereading: this note reminds you of that one. You can now <mark>link your notes together</mark> — and find the thread again next time you open either one.

Three ways to link: from the "Linked notes" section below each note, by selecting several notes at once in your archives, or by pasting a note's link straight into another note's text — it becomes a clickable link. Links work both ways, never delete anything, and stay private: they never appear on your published posts.

In the same spirit, you can create <mark>tasks straight from a note</mark>: you're preparing a meeting, to-dos emerge — create them without leaving the note. They inherit its projects and keep a visible link back to their original context.

## A true space for writing

The note editor grew into a proper writing space:

- **Full screen**: one click on ⤢ and everything fades away — just your text and the capture bar, for the ideas that strike mid-sentence.
- **Outline**: on long, structured notes, thin marks appear on the right — one per heading. Hover, the outline unfolds; click, you're there.
- **The right editor, always**: creating a note now takes you straight to the full-page editor. QuickNotes keep their lightning-fast capture.
- **Also**: sort your archives by date (created, edited, published, archived), print any note in a clean layout, and full-width notes on mobile.

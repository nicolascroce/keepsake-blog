Open a book annotated by a good reader: the margins overflow. Reactions, objections, cross-references — a whole layer of thinking that accompanies the text without ever blending into it. That is exactly what Keepsake gives you today: <mark>margins for your notes</mark>.

## Material, not text

A note in progress attracts all sorts of things that don't belong inside it: the email excerpt you'll use to rewrite a paragraph tomorrow, the reference to double-check, the idea that isn't ripe yet. Until now you had to choose: write them into the text — and risk publishing your scratch thoughts — or lose them.

Comments settle this. Select a passage, click the bubble, drop your material: it lives **alongside** the text, anchored to the right spot. Rewrite the note as much as you like — the comment follows. And if the passage disappears entirely, nothing is lost: the comment keeps its original excerpt and moves to the note level.

Above all, <mark>a comment is never published</mark>: your Keepsake Page, your WordPress posts and your newsletters only ever show the text. The margin stays yours.

## Made to disappear

A margin note is not an archive. You reread, copy what matters, delete the rest — and whatever deserves to last becomes a note or a linked task. No "resolved" state, no guilt counter: <mark>comments are scaffolding, and scaffolding comes down when the wall stands</mark>.

## A margin you read at a glance

The note outline has been redesigned to carry this margin: one quiet vertical thread, dashes for your headings, **beads** for your comments — each at the level of its section. Hover the thread: labels reveal themselves in a wave under your cursor. Click: you land on the passage, highlighted, with the comment open beside it. Prefer to see everything at once? Pin the full panel open with one click.

## Two inks

If an AI assistant works on your notes — through the API or [the MCP server](https://keepsake.place/en/ai), which gains four dedicated tools — it can read your margin and leave its own remarks. Its comments appear in <mark>blue</mark>, yours in red. Your text stays untouched; the margin becomes the space where you think together: "review my draft and leave your remarks in the margin" becomes a real workflow — and you keep the pen.
